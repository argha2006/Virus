# Resume-based candidate assessment

Read the current user-designated resume for each search. Locate it among available files or authorized sources; do not substitute another version silently. The resume is one discovery input and the evidence source for candidate assessment, not a mandatory filter for the separate role-family search. If inaccessible, continue searching the target role families, disclose that candidate fit is unverified, and use only evidence supplied for the current task. Ask for the missing source when needed for assessment; do not block role-family discovery or present it as resume-verified.

Keep candidate evidence within the current task. Do not write personal identifiers, employment history, project facts, certifications, tenure or qualification gaps into this skill's files. Do not transmit the resume or contact details to search engines.

## Evidence to assess

- Overall experience versus domain, platform, module and product-version experience; do not double-count overlapping assignments.
- Responsibilities actually performed: requirements, design, configuration, integration, implementation, support, governance, process ownership or operations leadership.
- Scope: project workstream, enterprise architecture, global rollout, business process or direct operating function.
- Industry and planning context: manufacturing, service/spares, demand, supply, capacity, inventory and production scheduling.
- Leadership evidence: delivery leadership, stakeholder governance, direct reports and budget authority are distinct.
- Required credentials, languages and implementation counts, using only explicit evidence.

Distinguish hands-on delivery from training, demonstrations, exploration and adjacent exposure. Do not treat earlier product versions as proof of tenure in a later version. Consulting delivery does not itself prove factory operations ownership. A client assignment is not direct employment at that client.

## Fit decisions

First record why a role is relevant: resume overlap, target-role-family responsibilities or both. Then assess the candidate separately. Missing resume evidence does not make a substantively relevant role irrelevant; show it as a role-family opportunity or stretch with the gap stated. Explicit user exclusions still apply.

Separate mandatory requirements from preferred knowledge and accepted alternatives. Label unsupported mandatory requirements as gaps; treat a platform learning need differently when equivalent experience is explicitly accepted. Do not infer expertise merely from related modules or tools.

Use qualitative outcomes: direct fit supported by evidence, transferable fit with explained differences, stretch where substantial mandatory evidence is missing, or candidate fit unverified when evidence is unavailable. Do not discard a target-role match solely because its candidate assessment is stretch or unverified. Avoid invented match percentages. Explain the target responsibility match and material candidate gap rather than reproducing the resume.

Compare role seniority with actual duties and the resume. A bounded experience range can indicate a level mismatch; a lower minimum or unstated tenure does not establish one by itself. Do not infer location, salary, mobility or work-authorization preferences from career history.
